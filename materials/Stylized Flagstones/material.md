# Stylized Flagstones

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: irregular pale-gray flagstones set into recessed sandy mortar, with varied polygonal shapes, rounded chipped edges, readable height separation, and a softly stylized worn-stone finish. The pattern should feel hand-laid and organic rather than generated from a regular paving grid.

## Material identity

The surface is a dense mosaic of uneven stone slabs ranging from small fragments to broad dominant flags. Most stones are cool light gray or warm gray-beige, while the joints are muted sand, khaki, and gray-brown. Each slab rises clearly above the mortar, with softened bevels, chipped silhouettes, shallow surface waviness, fine pores, and occasional hairline cracks. The stylization comes from clean readable shapes and slightly emphasized edge relief, not cartoon color or uniform outlines.

## Color and pattern

- Center the stone palette on pale gray, warm gray, soft taupe, and subdued ivory.
- Vary stones individually and internally with restrained cloudy patches, faint darker rims, and small gray-green or brown stains.
- Use muted sand, beige-gray, and khaki for the recessed mortar, with darker compacted dirt near stone edges.
- Arrange irregular angular and rounded polygons at strongly varied sizes, avoiding rows, courses, or a repeating cobblestone grid.
- Include a few split stones, small wedge-shaped fragments, and incomplete clusters so the layout feels assembled by hand.
- Add sparse dark pores, tiny mineral flecks, faint moss-gray specks, and occasional small cream pebbles in wider joints.
- Avoid uniform stone colors, equally sized cells, perfect Voronoi polygons, dark graphic outlines, vivid moss, and high-contrast checker patterns.

## Surface response

The material is dry, matte, and fully non-metallic. Stone tops should have moderately high roughness with subtle variation from wear, pores, and stains. Mortar should be slightly rougher and more diffuse, while a few smoothed stone faces may catch a broader soft highlight. Nothing should appear wet, polished, waxed, or metallic.

## Relief and normals

Relief is pronounced at the structural level: slabs stand above recessed mortar with rounded, chipped, and uneven beveled edges. Vary stone height slightly so adjacent pieces do not form a perfectly flat plane. Add low-amplitude waviness, fine pitting, short scratches, shallow cracks, and localized edge damage to stone faces. Mortar should contain fine sandy grain and gentle depressions. Avoid vertical cliff edges, uniformly inflated stones, razor-sharp borders, deep canyon joints, or strong displacement on every small color variation.

## Scale and tiling

The pattern should contain a clear hierarchy of large, medium, and small flagstones, with wider and narrower joints weaving naturally between them. Large stones must not repeat conspicuously when viewed at 2x or 4x tiling. Every channel must tile seamlessly without clipped border stones, mirrored seams, recurring hero slabs, straight joint bands, or a centered radial composition.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as stylized, hand-laid irregular flagstone paving.
- Preserves the references' pale stone palette, sandy recessed mortar, and strong size variation.
- Shows rounded chipped edges and convincing stone-to-mortar height separation.
- Includes restrained pores, stains, small cracks, and occasional joint pebbles.
- Keeps surfaces dry, matte, non-metallic, and softly weathered.
- Remains seamless and non-repetitive at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward regular cobblestone, brick paving, cracked concrete, terrazzo, sharp Voronoi cells, or loose gravel.
