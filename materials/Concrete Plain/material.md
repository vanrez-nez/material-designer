# Concrete Plain

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: plain pale-gray concrete with restrained cement mottling, soft overlapping trowel swirls, faint wipe marks, and an extremely fine sandy surface. The result should feel clean, neutral, and utilitarian while retaining enough subtle variation to avoid looking like flat gray paint.

## Material identity

The concrete is predominantly light neutral gray with gentle shifts into slightly warmer and cooler gray. Broad, low-contrast cement clouds overlap across the surface, while hand-finishing leaves faint circular and arcing trowel traces. Fine grain and tiny pores give the material a dry tactile quality, but the surface remains continuous and free of exposed aggregate, large cavities, cracks, or obvious damage.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on pale neutral gray, light cement gray, soft ash, and muted warm gray.
- Add broad cloudy tonal variation with a narrow value range and very soft transitions.
- Include diffuse medium-gray trowel swirls, curved wipe marks, and occasional short smudges at low contrast.
- Layer fine mottled cement grain and sparse tiny dark pores without producing uniform speckling.
- Allow subtle warmer gray-beige traces in a few areas, but keep the overall appearance cool and neutral.
- Keep all markings subdued so no individual stain, swirl, or patch becomes a dominant feature.
- Avoid strong white deposits, charcoal blotches, exposed stones, hard-edged stains, repeated rings, and obvious procedural noise.

## Surface response

The finish is dry and matte with high, fairly even roughness. Hand-compressed trowel areas may be only slightly smoother, creating soft broad highlights rather than distinct shine. Roughness variation should be subtle and follow the underlying cement grain and tool marks. The material is fully non-metallic and must not appear wet, polished, chalk-coated, or plastic.

## Relief and normals

Relief is very shallow. Use low-amplitude broad undulation, faint trowel arcs, fine sandy grain, and sparse microscopic pinholes. Most of the visible pattern should come from restrained color and roughness variation rather than height. Preserve a smooth silhouette and avoid raised ridges, coarse aggregate, deep pores, chips, cracks, or rocky displacement.

## Scale and tiling

Several broad tonal clouds and overlapping trowel passes should cross the visible object, with fine grain remaining visible only at closer range. At mid distance the surface should read as calm, nearly uniform pale concrete. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, repeated circular swirls, obvious bands, or a centered patch.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as plain, clean, pale-gray concrete.
- Preserves the references' narrow tonal range and subtle overlapping trowel marks.
- Shows very fine cement grain and sparse pores without coarse aggregate or damage.
- Has a dry, high-roughness response with minimal variation and extremely shallow relief.
- Remains non-metallic, seamless, and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward plaster, stone, terrazzo, dirty concrete, polished cement, or flat featureless paint.
