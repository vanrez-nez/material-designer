# Rocky Ground

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: compacted dark rocky soil with a dense fine-grain matrix, pale mineral grit, and sparse clusters of larger angular stones. The ground should feel dry, worn, and naturally accumulated rather than like evenly distributed decorative gravel or exposed concrete aggregate.

## Material identity

The base is dense brown-gray earth or crushed mineral fines, predominantly charcoal-brown, muted olive-gray, and dusty taupe. Countless tiny pale grains are embedded throughout, while larger cool-gray rock fragments appear in irregular clusters, trails, and isolated pieces. Most of the surface is relatively flat and compacted, with local disturbed areas where stones sit slightly proud or shallowly embedded.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on dark gray-brown, charcoal taupe, muted olive-brown, and dusty earth gray.
- Build broad, low-contrast compacted-soil fields with soft mottling rather than a flat uniform fill.
- Scatter dense, tiny pale beige, off-white, and light-gray mineral grains throughout the surface.
- Mix in sparse charcoal and near-black grains to deepen the fine aggregate without creating harsh noise.
- Place medium and larger angular gray stones in irregular, low-coverage clusters, short trails, and isolated fragments.
- Vary the stones between cool gray, dusty warm gray, and subdued brown-gray, with occasional lighter fractured faces.
- Keep the rock clusters organically spaced and locally concentrated; large parts of the ground should remain dominated by fine compacted material.
- Avoid a uniform pebble distribution, strong salt-and-pepper contrast, repeated clusters, circular crater patterns, vivid colors, and obvious procedural cells.

## Surface response

The material is dry, matte, non-metallic, and highly rough. The compacted soil may read slightly smoother than the loose grit, but it should retain a diffuse, dusty response. Individual rock fragments and mineral grains can vary subtly in roughness without producing wet highlights, polished faces, or glitter-like sparkle.

## Relief and normals

Use continuous fine sandy and gritty microrelief across the ground matrix, supported by very gentle broad undulation. Tiny grains should create restrained surface breakup rather than each becoming a pronounced bump. Larger stones should rise modestly above the soil with angular, chipped, but weather-softened forms. Suggest shallow embedding depressions and small accumulations of grit around some clusters. Avoid deep holes, steep displacement, inflated pebble shapes, sharp spikes, and uniformly strong grain relief.

## Scale and tiling

Preserve a clear scale hierarchy: broad areas of mostly uninterrupted compacted soil, dense ubiquitous fine grit, scattered medium fragments, and a limited number of larger clustered stones. Rock sizes and cluster densities should vary naturally. The material must tile seamlessly without recognizable hero stones, repeated trails, or evenly spaced clusters when viewed at 1x, 2x, and 4x repetition.

## Acceptance targets

- Reads immediately as dry, compacted rocky ground rather than concrete, asphalt, or decorative gravel.
- Maintains a dark earthy matrix densely populated by tiny pale mineral grains.
- Includes sparse, irregular clusters of angular gray rock fragments with believable size variation.
- Combines high roughness with layered but controlled micro- and macro-relief.
- Remains non-metallic and seamless at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling scales.
- Avoids terrazzo, beach sand, uniform gravel beds, dense rubble, and stylized flagstone patterns.
