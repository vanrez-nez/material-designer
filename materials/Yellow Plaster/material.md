# Yellow Plaster

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: warm yellow-ochre plaster with broad hand-smoothed trowel patches, fine sandy aggregate, scattered chalky flecks, and sparse signs of wear. The color should remain muted and earthy, reading as naturally pigmented plaster rather than bright yellow paint.

## Material identity

The plaster has a warm sand, ochre, and beige body with subtle tonal variation from one trowel pass to another. Its surface alternates between softly compressed areas and slightly rougher granular patches. Small pits, pale aggregate grains, faint scratches, occasional hairline cracks, and a few isolated dark chips give it an aged handmade character without making it heavily damaged.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on muted yellow ochre, warm sand, pale tan, and creamy beige.
- Add broad, softly blended patches of lighter buff and darker dusty brown to describe overlapping plaster applications.
- Include sparse gray-brown smudges and gently darkened worn areas, keeping the overall surface light and warm.
- Scatter tiny chalk-white and pale stone-colored aggregate flecks at varied sizes and densities.
- Add very sparse rust-red, umber, and charcoal grains or chips as small accents rather than a uniform speckle field.
- Let subtle trowel arcs and broad wipe marks influence the tonal breakup without becoming hard-edged bands.
- Avoid lemon yellow, orange saturation, flat painted color, uniform noise, repeated circular stains, and obvious procedural clouds.

## Surface response

The finish is dry and matte, with high overall roughness and only soft, broad highlights. Slightly compressed trowel patches may be marginally smoother than the surrounding sandy plaster, while chalky aggregate, pits, and worn marks remain rougher. The material is non-metallic and should not appear glossy, wet, waxed, or plastic.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow and finely layered. Use broad low-amplitude undulation for overlapping trowel passes, medium detail for soft scrape ridges and worn patches, and fine detail for sandy grain, tiny protruding aggregate, pinholes, and short scratches. Add only a few thin, broken hairline cracks and occasional small chipped cavities. Avoid deep fissures, heavy erosion, sharp stone-like displacement, and evenly distributed bumps.

## Scale and tiling

Several broad light and dark trowel regions should cross the visible object, with fine grit and small imperfections distributed inside them. The plaster should read calm and mostly continuous at mid distance while revealing aggregate and tool marks up close. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, obvious repeated chips, long continuous cracks, or a centered stain.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as warm, naturally pigmented yellow-ochre plaster.
- Preserves the references' muted sandy palette and broad hand-troweled variation.
- Includes fine pale aggregate, sparse colored grains, small pits, and limited wear.
- Has a dry high-roughness response with shallow, believable plaster relief.
- Remains non-metallic, seamless, and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward painted drywall, sandstone, concrete, stucco, mud, rust, or brightly saturated yellow.
