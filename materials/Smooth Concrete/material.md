# Smooth Concrete

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: dense, smoothed gray concrete with broad cloudy tonal variation, shallow rubbed tool texture, fine pores, and sparse chalky deposits. “Smooth” describes the continuous finished surface and lack of exposed coarse aggregate; it should still retain fine tactile irregularity and age.

## Material identity

The concrete is predominantly medium cool gray with overlapping lighter and darker cement clouds. Subtle warm taupe and brown-gray traces break up the neutral base, while small pale mineral deposits and fine dark pores add age and material depth. The surface appears hand-finished or lightly troweled, with broad compressed patches and shallow irregular texture rather than formwork lines or polished sheen.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on medium cool gray, slate gray, ash gray, and soft charcoal.
- Add broad cloudy regions of pale cement gray and deeper blue-gray with soft, eroded transitions.
- Introduce restrained warm taupe, dusty brown, and beige-gray discoloration in scattered patches.
- Include sparse chalk-white mineral blooms and small pale deposits, keeping them irregular and low coverage.
- Add fine dark pinholes, tiny cement grains, and mottled specks without creating uniform salt-and-pepper noise.
- Build the pattern in nested scales: broad cement clouds, medium rubbed or troweled patches, fine granular breakup, and tiny pores.
- Avoid flat monochrome gray, large exposed stones, sharp stains, repeated cells, dramatic black patches, and obvious procedural clouds.

## Surface response

The finish is dry and matte to softly satin. Roughness should be moderately high overall, with subtle variation tied to compacted patches, porous regions, and mineral deposits. Smoothed areas may catch a broad subdued highlight, but the surface must not look glossy, wet, waxed, or polished. The material is fully non-metallic.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow and continuous. Use broad low-amplitude undulation for the hand-finished cement surface, medium detail for rubbed trowel patches and small irregular depressions, and fine detail for pinholes, sandy grain, and scattered raised mineral residue. Keep the silhouette smooth and avoid deep cavities, large chips, exposed aggregate, strong ridges, or rocky displacement. Color variation should remain more prominent than height variation.

## Scale and tiling

Several broad light and dark cement regions should cross the visible object, with fine pores and tool texture distributed inside them. The concrete should read as a calm continuous surface at mid distance while retaining tactile detail up close. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, repeated blotches, directional bands, or a dominant centered patch.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as dense, smoothed, aged gray concrete.
- Preserves the references' cool gray cement clouds, warm traces, and sparse chalky deposits.
- Shows fine pores and shallow rubbed texture without exposed coarse aggregate.
- Has a dry matte-to-satin response with restrained roughness variation.
- Keeps displacement shallow and the overall silhouette smooth.
- Remains non-metallic, seamless, and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward plaster, natural stone, asphalt, terrazzo, polished cement, or heavily eroded concrete.
