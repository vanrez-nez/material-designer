# Coper Old

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: old dark copper with deep brown-black oxidation, subdued rose-copper undertones, a reflective rubbed finish, dense fine scratches, and shallow pebbled wear. The surface should feel like aged solid metal that has been handled and abraded over time, without green verdigris or bright orange corrosion.

## Material identity

The metal is predominantly very dark brown, smoky umber, and near-black copper oxide. Warm muted copper, rose-brown, and faint plum tones remain visible beneath the oxidation, especially where broad highlights reveal the metallic substrate. The surface combines softly polished worn areas with rougher oxidized patches, fine multidirectional abrasion, shallow dents, and small pits.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on dark chocolate brown, blackened umber, deep bronze-brown, and muted rose copper.
- Use broad, softly blended oxidation fields that shift between nearly black areas and warmer exposed copper-brown regions.
- Add restrained smoky plum, red-brown, and desaturated mauve undertones where the copper shows through.
- Include subtle dark smudges, rubbed patches, and small oxidized pits without creating painted black blotches.
- Keep color contrast subordinate to the metal's reflective response; much of the visible light-dark range should come from lighting and roughness rather than base-color graphics.
- Avoid green or turquoise verdigris, vivid orange rust, bright polished penny copper, uniform soot, repeated cells, and obvious cloudy noise.

## Surface response

The material is metallic throughout. It should produce strong, broad reflections with a warm copper tint, but the aged finish prevents mirror-like clarity. Roughness should vary from moderately low on rubbed high points to medium-high in oxidized, pitted regions. Variation should be broad and softly layered, with finer changes tied to scratches and pebbled texture. Do not use dielectric white highlights or make the surface look wet, painted, or coated in plastic.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow but clearly visible in highlights. Use broad low-amplitude waviness, medium pebbled or lightly hammered unevenness, scattered shallow dents and pinholes, and dense fine abrasion. Scratches should be thin, short to medium length, irregularly overlapping, and oriented in many directions, with occasional clustered scuff zones. They should primarily affect normals and roughness rather than appearing as dark drawn lines in base color. Avoid deep gouges, regular hammer dimples, parallel machining grooves, or sharply displaced corrosion crust.

## Scale and tiling

Broad oxidized and rubbed regions should span the visible object, while fine scratches, pits, and pebbled relief remain legible inside them. At mid distance the surface should read as dark reflective aged copper; at close range it should reveal accumulated wear. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, repeated scratch clusters, obvious bands, or a dominant centered highlight pattern.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as dark, old, heavily handled copper metal.
- Preserves the references' brown-black oxidation and subdued rose-copper undertones.
- Produces strong warm metallic reflections softened by varied aged roughness.
- Shows shallow pebbled wear, small pits, and dense multidirectional scratches.
- Keeps scratches primarily in normal and roughness response rather than painted into base color.
- Remains metallic, seamless, and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward iron, steel, black plastic, leather, rusted orange metal, or green-patinated copper.
