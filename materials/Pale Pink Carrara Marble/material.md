# Pale Pink Carrara Marble

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: a polished pale pink marble with broad dusty-rose mineral masses, cool blue-gray fields, dark charcoal inclusions, delicate branching seams, and dense fine mineral breakup. Despite the Carrara name, the reference is not predominantly white; it should retain its muted pink-gray identity.

## Material identity

The stone has a compact, brecciated appearance formed by overlapping irregular mineral regions. Large blush and mauve-gray areas interlock with cooler slate-gray and charcoal patches, while pale pink calcite seams wind through their boundaries. The structure should feel naturally fractured, compressed, and softened by mineral deposition rather than sharply cracked or assembled from discrete tiles.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on desaturated dusty pink, blush-gray, mauve, and warm pale rose.
- Balance the pink regions with broad cool blue-gray and medium neutral-gray mineral fields.
- Add irregular charcoal-gray and near-black inclusions with softened, eroded edges; avoid flat solid-black shapes.
- Use pale pink, gray-white, and subdued ivory for narrow branching seams, small cloudy deposits, and occasional diffuse patches.
- Build the pattern in nested scales: broad interlocking masses, medium fractured islands, narrow mineral boundaries, fine granular mottling, and tiny light and dark specks.
- Let some seams branch or form incomplete loops, but keep them broken, varied, and subordinate to the larger mineral structure.
- Avoid a white Carrara base, long isolated ribbon veins, uniform terrazzo speckles, obvious cells, high-saturation pink, and repeated procedural blobs.

## Surface response

The finish is polished and glossy-honed, producing a strong broad highlight while preserving the darker mineral pattern beneath it. Roughness should remain low overall with subtle variation linked to the fine mineral texture and seams. The stone is non-metallic and should read as dense polished marble rather than wet concrete, plastic, chalk, or porous rock.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow and the silhouette should remain smooth. Broad color fields must not become raised terrain. Use restrained normal variation for fine mineral grain, tiny pits, and narrow seam edges. The references also show faint multidirectional polishing scratches inside highlights; these should be very thin, irregular, low-contrast normal or roughness details and must not appear as drawn lines in the base color.

## Scale and tiling

Several large pink and blue-gray mineral regions should span the visible object, with numerous medium fragments and fine speckles nested inside them. The pattern should remain dense and legible at close range without collapsing into uniform noise at a distance. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, conspicuous repeated hero shapes, or a centered composition.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as polished dusty-pink and blue-gray natural marble.
- Preserves the references' dense interlocking mineral structure and restrained mauve palette.
- Includes softened charcoal inclusions and broken pale branching seams at multiple scales.
- Shows a broad polished highlight with fine mineral and hairline surface breakup.
- Keeps relief shallow, coherent, and subordinate to the color pattern.
- Remains non-metallic, seamless, and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward white Carrara, terrazzo, granite, concrete, camouflage, or brightly saturated rose stone.
