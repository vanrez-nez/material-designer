# Verdi Almi Marble

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: a polished green-gray marble with broad cloudy mineral fields, pale calcite veining, fine internal mottling, and subtle surface wear. The stone should feel dense and softly layered rather than crystalline, speckled, or sharply fractured.

## Material identity

The marble is dominated by muted gray-green, sage, and olive tones. Large, diffuse mineral clouds overlap across the surface, interrupted by milky off-white and pale gray calcite regions. The pattern has a gentle directional flow in places, but no single continuous vein or rigid grain direction should dominate the material.

## Color and pattern

- Use a restrained, low-saturation palette of gray-green, sage, olive-gray, cool gray, and soft ivory.
- Build broad, softly blended green mineral masses with irregular pale calcite clouds and occasional narrow wisps.
- Add medium-scale feathery streaks and eroded, branching transitions between light and dark regions.
- Layer fine granular mottling and small translucent-looking mineral flecks inside the larger forms.
- Keep contrast moderate: the pale areas should be luminous and cloudy, not pure white, while the darkest areas remain softened green-gray rather than black.
- Avoid crisp graphic veins, uniform spots, repeated cells, strong emerald color, hard black cracks, and obvious procedural repetition.

## Surface response

The finish is polished and reflective, producing broad, bright highlights with a slightly softened edge. Roughness should be low overall with restrained, cloudy variation tied to the mineral structure. The stone is non-metallic and should retain a deep, smooth dielectric response rather than looking wet, plastic, chalky, or porous.

## Relief and normals

Relief is extremely shallow. Broad veins and mineral clouds are primarily color variation and should not become raised terrain. Use subtle normal detail for fine mineral grain and faint hairline polishing scratches visible inside highlights. The scratches should be sparse, thin, irregular, and overlapping in varied directions; they must affect surface reflection only and must not appear as dark lines in the base color.

## Scale and tiling

Several broad cloudy regions should cross the visible object, with finer streaks and mineral breakup remaining legible at close range. Preserve the soft layered flow across curved surfaces. Every channel must tile seamlessly without mirrored borders, abrupt seams, repeated hero shapes, or a centered pattern.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as polished muted green marble.
- Preserves the references' broad milky clouds, soft green-gray veining, and fine mineral texture.
- Shows strong polished highlights without losing the underlying pattern.
- Keeps height nearly flat, with only delicate mineral grain and normal-only hairline wear.
- Remains non-metallic, smooth, seamless, and convincing at both close and mid distance.
- Does not drift toward granite, terrazzo, concrete, jade, moss, or sharply cracked stone.
