# Moss Green Solid Granite

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: a dense, polished moss-green granite with a complex, naturally mottled mineral structure. The name refers to the stone's muted green coloration; the surface must not contain literal moss, vegetation, dirt, or fuzzy growth.

## Material identity

The stone is predominantly desaturated sage, olive-gray, and warm gray. It contains overlapping, irregular mineral fields rather than distinct large crystals or evenly distributed speckles. Broad cloudy regions should break into smaller interlocking fragments, pale mineral rims, tiny pits, and scattered dark inclusions. The overall appearance is compact and ancient, with considerable internal variation but no strong directional grain.

## Color and pattern

- Use a restrained low-saturation palette centered on medium olive-gray and sage-gray.
- Mix broad cool gray-green areas with warmer khaki, taupe, and muted brown patches.
- Add irregular off-white, pale beige, and light gray mineral boundaries and small deposits.
- Include sparse charcoal-brown flecks and pinholes at several sizes. They should support the stone structure without turning it into a uniform salt-and-pepper granite.
- Build the pattern in nested scales: large cloudy masses, medium amoeba-like mineral islands, fine granular breakup, and very small pores.
- Keep transitions mostly organic and softly eroded, with occasional thin, brighter, sharper mineral edges.
- Avoid obvious cells, repeated blobs, straight veins, evenly sized dots, strong green saturation, and high-contrast black-and-white noise.

## Surface response

The reference finish is polished to glossy-honed. It produces a broad, clear highlight while the underlying color remains visible. Roughness should therefore be low to moderately low overall, with subtle variation tied to mineral regions. Pale deposits and tiny pores may be slightly rougher, but the material must still read as polished stone rather than chalk, concrete, or wet slime.

The material is non-metallic. Use dielectric reflectance throughout. Ambient occlusion should be restrained and limited to the smallest pits and mineral recesses; it must not dirty the broad color pattern.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow. The stone silhouette should remain smooth, and the large color fields should not become inflated terrain. Use fine normal variation for crystalline grain, minute pits, and narrow mineral boundaries, with only a very gentle contribution from medium-scale structure. Height should remain subtle enough that direct highlights reveal texture without making the slab look eroded or porous.

## Scale and tiling

At the scale shown on the reference sphere and cylinder, several broad mineral regions should cross the visible object while dozens of smaller fragments remain legible inside them. The pattern must remain convincing at both close and mid distance. It should tile seamlessly in every channel, without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, conspicuous repeated hero shapes, or an obvious dominant center.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as polished gray-green natural stone.
- Preserves the references' dense, layered mineral complexity and subdued palette.
- Shows broad specular highlights with believable fine breakup.
- Contains shallow, coherent normal and roughness detail rather than embossed color noise.
- Remains non-metallic and free of excessive cavity darkening.
- Looks seamless and non-repetitive at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward marble, terrazzo, concrete, camouflage, lichen, or literal moss.
