# Dirty Gray Plaster

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: aged gray plaster with a dry, heavily worked surface, broad uneven discoloration, embedded grime, and dense trowel texture. It should read as hand-applied wall plaster that has accumulated dirt and staining, not as bare concrete, stone, metal, or wet mud.

## Material identity

The base is a warm, low-saturation gray-beige plaster. Its surface is irregularly compacted and scraped, with overlapping trowel passes that create soft sweeping arcs, directional drag marks, and patches of differing smoothness. Darker dirt has settled unevenly across the wall, while muted olive and ochre traces suggest age, damp staining, and embedded environmental residue.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on warm gray, greige, dusty beige, and subdued taupe.
- Add broad cloudy fields of charcoal-gray and dirty brown, with softened irregular edges and varied opacity.
- Include restrained olive, khaki, and dull yellow-green staining in scattered areas; it should look mineral or environmental, not like vivid moss.
- Layer pale chalky gray patches where the plaster appears more worn, dry, or thinly spread.
- Add fine dark grit, tiny embedded specks, and occasional shallow pinholes without creating uniform salt-and-pepper noise.
- Let large discoloration fields overlap with medium smudges and fine granular breakup so the dirt feels accumulated rather than procedurally sprayed.
- Avoid hard-edged stains, isolated circular splats, obvious cells, repeated clouds, painted camouflage, and high-saturation green or yellow.

## Surface response

The finish is dry and strongly matte. Roughness should be high overall, with subtle local variation: compacted trowel ridges may catch a slightly tighter highlight, while chalky deposits, grit, and dirty recesses remain rougher. The material is non-metallic and should scatter light softly without glossy reflections or a wet sheen.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow but continuously present. Use layered normal and height detail for broad trowel undulation, medium overlapping scrape arcs, fine directional drag marks, granular plaster aggregate, and scattered tiny pits. Trowel marks should change direction and length across the surface rather than forming parallel grooves. Keep the relief believable for a thin plaster coat: textured and tactile, but not deeply eroded, rocky, cracked, or heavily chipped.

## Scale and tiling

Several broad dirty and cleaner regions should cross the visible object, with many overlapping hand-worked marks inside them. Fine grit and drag texture should remain visible up close while merging into a coherent matte surface at mid distance. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, repeated circular swirls, obvious vertical bands, or a dominant centered stain.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as aged, dirty, hand-troweled gray plaster.
- Preserves the references' warm gray-beige body, smoky grime, and restrained olive staining.
- Shows varied overlapping trowel arcs and fine drag marks rather than uniform noise.
- Has a dry, high-roughness response with shallow granular relief and small pits.
- Remains non-metallic, seamless, and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward concrete, natural stone, rusted metal, mud, moss, cracked stucco, or glossy polished plaster.
