# Cast Iron Damaged

Create a seamless procedural material matching the supplied references: blackened cast iron with a dark satin sheen, fine pebbled casting texture, broad uneven oxidation, and scattered reddish-brown corrosion. The damage should remain shallow and surface-bound, suggesting worn and partially oxidized iron rather than deeply flaking or structurally broken metal.

## Material identity

The iron is predominantly near-black, charcoal, and dark gunmetal gray. Broad patches vary between smoother exposed metal and darker oxidized areas, while muted red-brown corrosion appears as irregular stains, clustered specks, and softly eroded islands. A dense fine casting grain covers the surface and becomes most visible where broad highlights rake across it.

## Color and pattern

- Center the palette on black, charcoal gray, dark graphite, and subdued gunmetal.
- Add broad softly blended regions of slightly lighter gray metal and deeper soot-black oxidation.
- Introduce muted rust-brown, burnt umber, and dark red-orange corrosion in irregular low-coverage patches.
- Break the rust into layered scales: broad faint staining, medium eroded islands, and scattered fine oxidized specks.
- Include sparse pale gray rubbed spots where the dark surface has thinned, without creating bright silver chips.
- Keep transitions mottled and diffuse, with occasional sharper broken edges around small damaged areas.
- Avoid vivid orange rust, uniform brown coverage, painted camouflage, repeated cells, hard circular stains, and clean polished steel coloration.

## Surface response

Exposed iron should retain a metallic response with a broad satin highlight. Roughness should be moderate and varied: smoother rubbed regions reflect more clearly, while black oxide and reddish corrosion are rougher and less reflective. Corroded oxide areas may reduce metallic response because they are no longer clean exposed metal, but this variation should remain tied precisely to the rust masks. The surface must not look wet, chrome-like, glossy plastic, or uniformly matte.

## Relief and normals

Relief is shallow and densely textured. Use fine pebbled casting grain across the whole material, with low-amplitude broad waviness and subtle clusters of tiny pits. Rust patches may be slightly roughened or recessed, but should not form thick crusts, deep cavities, sharp flakes, or cracked scales. Preserve a smooth overall silhouette while allowing raking highlights to reveal the granular metal surface.

## Scale and tiling

Several broad dark and slightly lighter metal regions should cross the visible object, with medium rust clusters and many fine casting pores nested inside them. At mid distance the material should read as blackened damaged iron; at close range the casting grain and shallow oxidation should become apparent. Every channel must tile seamlessly without border discontinuities, mirrored seams, repeated rust islands, directional bands, or a dominant centered stain.

## Acceptance targets

- Immediately reads as blackened, worn cast iron.
- Preserves the references' near-black body, gunmetal highlights, and subdued red-brown oxidation.
- Shows broad satin reflections broken by fine pebbled casting grain.
- Uses rougher, less-metallic response only where corrosion and oxide are present.
- Keeps damage shallow, mottled, and free of exaggerated crust or deep erosion.
- Remains seamless and convincing at 1x, 2x, and 4x tiling.
- Does not drift toward painted black plastic, polished steel, bronze, leather, volcanic rock, or heavily rusted orange iron.
